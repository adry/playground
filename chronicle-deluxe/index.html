How the house works

From a problem we know to a business that pays its way.

Every venture goes through the same four rooms. Some take a weekend. Some take a few seasons.

  1. 01

    Spot

    We start with problems we have lived. In fashion, that is nineteen years of things that should work better. Everywhere else, it is whatever slows us down every day: if we keep hitting the same wall, we build the fix, and if the fix works for us, it might be a business.

  2. 02

    Build

    Small team, sharp tools. AI lets us go from idea to App Store in a weekend and from pilot to product in a season. We test with real customers before we fall in love with anything.

  3. 03

    Run

    Every venture has to stand on its own margins. We operate what we build: the support inbox, the wholesale season, the late-night integration fix. No burn-for-growth theatre.

  4. 04

    Compound

    What one venture learns, the next one inherits. Code, playbooks, suppliers, customers and taste flow between them, so each new business starts further ahead than the last.

House rules

Six rules, pinned to the fridge.

  1. 01

    Margins over moonshots.

    Every venture has to pay its own way. Growth is great. Gross margin is greater.

  2. 02

    Operators, not observers.

    We have run the warehouse, the PLM rollout and the wholesale season. We build from the floor up.

  3. 03

    Be our own first customer.

    Outside fashion, every venture here began as a fix for our own daily problem. We use it every day before anyone else pays for it.

  4. 04

    Ship it, then polish it.

    A weekend prototype in a customer's hands beats a quarter of slideware.

  5. 05

    Taste is a feature.

    The back office deserves good design too. So does the invoice, the onboarding and the error message.

  6. 06

    Share the toolbox.

    Code, playbooks and lessons move freely between ventures. Each one makes the next one faster.

Who runs the house

Fashion operator. Accidental engineer.

Chronicle Deluxe is run by Adrien Forray, who spent nineteen years inside fashion operations, from luxury houses like LVMH, Kering and Burberry to Scandinavia's contemporary brands. Raw materials, sourcing, supply chain, ERP and PLM rollouts, the lot.

He failed C++ in 2003. In 2026 he shipped an iOS app in a weekend. The house exists in the gap between those two facts: deep industry know-how, finally paired with tools that let a small team build what it used to only spec.

FAQ

Fair questions.

What is Chronicle Deluxe?

A multidisciplinary business incubator. We build and run our own companies in fashion, AI, software and iOS apps, and we take on transformation and AI projects for other businesses.

Can I hire you?

Yes. The fashion transformation consultancy helps brands fix operations, supply chain and systems like ERP and PLM. The AI implementation practice builds agents, automations and internal tools that go into production and stay there.

Where do your ideas come from?

Two places. The fashion ventures come from nineteen years inside the industry. Everything else comes from our own daily problems: when something slows us down again and again, we build the fix, use it ourselves, and turn it into a business once it works.

What kind of businesses do you incubate?

Ones that solve a problem we have lived from the inside, that a small team with good tools can build fast, and that can stand on their own margins. Today that spans PLM software, B2B wholesale, menswear and iOS apps. Tomorrow, who knows.

How do I pitch an idea or a partnership?

Email hello@chronicledeluxe.com with the problem, who has it, and why now. Every message gets read, usually with coffee.
